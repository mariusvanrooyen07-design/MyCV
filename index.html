About me

I'm Marius van Rooyen, a full-time programming and web development student currently completing the HyperionDev bootcamp. Before making that switch, I spent years as an e-Learning Development Officer at Aros, designing and building digital learning experiences for higher education. My academic background is unusually varied: I hold a Bachelor of Theology and an Honours degree in Ancient Languages (Semitic languages) from North-West University, alongside more recent, practical qualifications in web design (HTML5, CSS3 and JavaScript) from the University of Cape Town and in Moodle-based e-learning development. That mix of theological training and technical skill shapes how I approach my work: I care as much about the substance of what is being taught as I do about the platform it's delivered through.

Over the years I've worked across the full spectrum of academic life — as a full-time theology student, an academic assistant, a part-time lecturer, and, most recently, as an e-learning developer building and maintaining online courses, modules and digital tools. I resigned from that role in February 2026 to commit myself fully to programming and web development, which I'm now studying full time. Before moving into higher education, I spent over a decade in business, progressing from admin clerk to accountant to Managing Director at a family owned brick manufacturing company, which gave me a solid grounding in operations, finance and leadership that I still draw on today. I've also presented at national conferences on technology in education and contributed to a published work on curriculum studies.

I'm deeply passionate about Christian higher education and about being part of the transformation of learning environments in South African schools and universities. My goal is to keep bridging the gap between educators and technology — making digital tools genuinely useful in the classroom rather than a barrier to good teaching. That's exactly why I made the leap into full-time programming study: I want to build more of the learning tools I used to have to rely on others for. Looking ahead, I'm focused on completing my development training and moving into a role — ideally in ed-tech — where I can combine my teaching background with hands-on coding skills to keep contributing to meaningful, mission-driven education in South Africa.
